Chapter 1
INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background of the Capstone Project
The barangays in the Philippines serve as the
smallest administrative units responsible for delivering basic
public services and managing shared community resources.
Among these responsibilities is the proper scheduling and
allocation of public facilities such as basketball courts,
multipurpose halls, covered courts, and community spaces.
Despite the importance of these services, many barangays
continue to rely on manual and informal processes for
managing facility reservations.
In Barangay Culiat, Quezon City, residents and staff
have historically coordinated facility use through walk-in
requests, phone messages, logbooks, and informal
communication channels. These methods often result in
delayed responses, inconsistent records, overlapping
bookings, and limited visibility of facility availability.
Residents may need to visit the barangay hall repeatedly to

-- 17 of 227 --

2
confirm schedules, while staff spend significant time
validating requests and resolving conflicts manually.
To address these challenges, the researchers
developed the AI-Driven Facilities Reservation System with
Predictive Scheduling Features for Barangay Culiat. The
system is a web-based PHP and MySQL application that
centralizes facility reservation workflows for residents,
barangay staff, and administrators. It supports online
booking, real-time availability checks, staff approval, AI-
assisted conflict detection and recommendations,
notifications, attendance tracking, and operational reporting.
The system was implemented as a modular
monolithic web application integrated with Python-based
machine learning services and Google Gemini chatbot
support for the dashboard. Its purpose is to improve
transparency, reduce scheduling conflicts, and provide
barangay personnel with a single platform for managing
public facility reservations.

public service management through the digital

Barangay Culiat experiences inefficiencies in

absence of a centralized digital reservation system.

2.1 Agile Scrum Methodology Overview
Agile Scrum is a software development methodology
that emphasizes iterative progress, collaboration, and
adaptability. Projects are divided into short cycles called
sprints, enabling teams to deliver functional features
incrementally and adjust based on user feedback. Defined
Scrum roles including the Product Owner, Scrum Master,
and Development Team ensure organized workflow,
accountability, and continuous improvement.
In the context of the AI-Driven Facilities Reservation
System for Barangay Culiat, Agile Scrum facilitated the rapid
development of core features such as real-time facility
availability, AI-assisted conflict detection and
recommendations, staff approval workflows, notifications,
and occupancy monitoring. Through iterative sprints, the
project team was able to incorporate stakeholder feedback,
refine system modules, and ensure that the platform adapts
effectively to the operational needs of residents and
barangay staff. This approach minimized delays, improved

-- 33 of 227 --

18
service delivery, and supported the scalability and
maintainability of the system.
2.2 Enterprise Architecture Concepts
Enterprise Architecture (EA) provides a structured
framework that ensures an information system aligns with
organizational processes, goals, and technology
infrastructure. It serves as a blueprint for how different
components interact, supporting efficiency, scalability, and
maintainability.

This design groups related functions into

7.3. Data Storage and Management
Figure no.43 ERD Diagram
All operational data is stored in a MySQL/MariaDB relational
database (utf8mb4, InnoDB) defined by a base schema and applied
incremental migrations. The entity relationship model centers on three

-- 142 of 227 --

127
entities: users, facilities, and reservations. A user may own many
reservations and a facility may be booked by many reservations, so the
reservations table carries foreign keys to both. Each reservation is further
linked to child records that capture its lifecycle: reservation_history (status
timeline), reservation_attendance and reservation_checkin_waivers
(check-in), and reservation_documents.
Supporting entities include notifications and user_violations (linked to
users), facility_blackout_dates and facility_equipment (linked to facilities),
and governance tables such as audit_log, security_logs, login_attempts,
and role_permissions. Integration caches (energy_facility_map,
energy_meter_readings, energy_recommendations_cache, and
energy_sync_state) store synchronized external data keyed to local
facilities. Referential integrity is enforced through foreign keys, and
uniqueness constraints (for example, one meter reading per facility per
month) prevent duplicate records
7.4. Data Synchronization Across Systems
The main application and the LGU Energy Management System
maintain separate databases—MySQL/MariaDB and SQLite,
respectively—and never share storage directly. Data crosses the boundary
only through the Energy REST API: the reservation system pushes
manually encoded meter readings and pulls facility mappings and

-- 143 of 227 --

128
engineer-approved recommendations, storing the results in local cache
tables(energy_facility_map,energy_meter_readings,energy_recommenda
tions_cache). Records are keyed by their remote identifiers and reconciled
using update timestamps, while a singleton energy_sync_state row tracks
the last successful push and pull. Synchronization runs either on demand
through an administrator’s “Sync Now” action or on a scheduled basis.
Maintenance data from CIMM follows the same pattern, with an
inbound webhook applying status changes as they happen, while IPMS
project data is polled in read-only mode. Within the main application itself,
no synchronization is needed: every module reads and writes to the same
MySQL database, and the Python machine-learning components train on
and read from that same database. As a result, reservation data remains
consistent across the web dashboards, the mobile API, and the AI
features.

8.3. Scalability and Performance
Considerations
Performance is addressed at the data layer through indexed,
constraint-protected tables and PDO prepared statements, and at the
interface layer through AJAX partial updates that refresh only the data that
changed instead of reloading full pages. External API results are cached
locally (for example, energy recommendations and synchronization state),
so dashboards never block on remote systems, and rate limiting with login-

-- 146 of 227 --

131
attempt throttling protects the application under abusive load. The
machine-learning models run as separate short-lived processes, keeping
heavy computation out of the web request path.
For scalability, the page scripts are stateless beyond the PHP
session, so the application can be scaled horizontally behind a load
balancer while the database scales independently through indexing and, if
needed, read replicas. The integrations are loosely coupled over REST,
allowing the energy, maintenance, and project systems to grow separately,
and the Python AI layer can be promoted from a process bridge to a
dedicated Flask REST service without changing the calling code’s
contract. Paginated queries and CDN-delivered frontend libraries keep
response times consistent as reservation volume grows.

12.4. Lessons Learned
During the development of the Barangay Culiat Facilities
Reservation System, the developers learned many valuable lessons in
planning, developing, testing, and documenting the system. One of the
most important lessons was making sure that the documentation always
matches the actual system. Every feature, workflow, and function should
be based on the implemented code and database to keep the
documentation accurate and reliable.
The project also showed the importance of testing the complete
system, especially when integrating with other systems. Although each
module worked properly on its own, some issues appeared during data

-- 186 of 227 --

171
sharing between integrated systems. This showed that end-to-end testing
is just as important as testing individual modules to make sure that all
connected systems work correctly.
Another important lesson was the value of continuous testing during
development. By using automated testing and continuous integration,
many errors were found and fixed early. This reduced debugging time and
helped improve the quality and stability of the system.
The developers also learned that the system should continue
working even if external services are temporarily unavailable. By adding
fallback solutions for third-party services, the system was still able to
provide its main functions without causing major problems for users.
Finally, the project highlighted the importance of security and data
privacy. Since the system handles residents' personal information and
uploaded documents, security features such as secure login, audit logs,
protected file storage, and compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012
were included from the beginning of development. This helped protect user
information and improved the overall reliability of the system.

organized into logical modules such as authentication, user management,
facility management, reservation management, notifications, artificial
intelligence assistance, reporting, and administration. This architecture
allows the system to maintain a simple deployment structure while
keeping its functional components organized and maintainable.
